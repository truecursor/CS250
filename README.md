## CS250: Reflection

### How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by looking at what the user is actually trying to accomplish, not just the feature they are asking for. For SNHU Travel project, the focus group feedback helped show what customers cared about, such as destinations, budget limits, and personalized travel choices. Creating user stories helped because it turned that feedback into clear development goals. Instead of just saying "make a travel app," the user stories broke the work into smaller pieces that could be prioritized, built, tested, and improved.

### How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach developing programs by starting with the big, main goal first, then breaking the work into smaller pieces that can actually be completed. I like the Agile process because it does not expect everything to be perfect at the start. For future development work, I want to use product backlogs, user stories, sprint planning, testing during development, and regular reviews. Those habits would help me stay organized while still being flexible when requirements change.

### What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member in software development means communicating clearly, being reliable, and helping the team reach the goal instead of only focusing on your own part. It also means being open to feedback and willing to adjust when priorities change. In Scrum, the team works better when the Product Owner, Scrum Master, developers, and testers all stay connected. A good team member does their work, asks questions early, shares blockers, and helps keep the project moving.
